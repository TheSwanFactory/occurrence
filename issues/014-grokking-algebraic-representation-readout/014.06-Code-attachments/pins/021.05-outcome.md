## 1. Exact `FFF` theorem

The executable audit checks all `64` ordered pairs in `V x V` and verifies:

```text
closure                 a XOR b in V
uniqueness              one c with a XOR b XOR c = 000
commutativity           a XOR b = b XOR a
associativity           (a XOR b) XOR c = a XOR (b XOR c)
self-inverse            a XOR a = 000
identity                a XOR 000 = a
direction-neutrality    a XOR b=c <=> a XOR c=b <=> b XOR c=a
```

For distinct nonzero `a,b`, `c=a XOR b` is nonzero and distinct. Unordered triples produce exactly:

```text
7 Fano lines,
21 distinct point-pairs,
exactly one line through every pair.
```

### 1.1 Relabeling


For `PG(2,2)` this reconstructs `C2^3` up to isomorphism.

## 3. Why the Pasch habitat is affine `F_2^2`

Fix a certified habitat `(P,delta)`. Its four cyclic blocks are the vertices of a `K4`; its six Events are the edges. The three Fano lines through `P` are the three opposite-edge perfect matchings.

Let

```text
Q_P = F_2^3 / <P>.
```

`Q_P` has four elements. Its three nonzero elements correspond naturally to the three Fano lines through `P`: a nonzero coset represented by `x` determines the line `{P,x,P XOR x}`.

Each opposite-edge matching defines a fixed-point-free involution on the four block vertices. The three matching involutions plus identity form

```text
C2^2 ~= Q_P.
```

Their action on the four blocks is free and transitive, so the block set is a `Q_P`-torsor. Choosing any origin gives two-bit coordinates

```text
PP=q in F_2^2.
```

A nonzero displacement `d` labels the Event joining

```text
q <-> q XOR d.
```

The same Event has the two incidence presentations

```text
(q,d) ~ (q XOR d,d).
```

Therefore one habitat has

```text
4*3/2 = 6 Events,
4*3   = 12 Event-block incidences,
4*3*2 = 24 ordered cyclic Event pairs.
```

Multiplying by fourteen gives the certified `84/168/336` counts.

The completed local value `pp=00` would be the identity translation / loop `q->q`. FIPS contains no such Event, so `pp=00` also remains formal completion only.

## 5. Admission versus consequence

For incidence presentations

```text
A=(S1,P1,q1,d1)
B=(S2,P2,q2,d2)
```

the finite cyclic relation is

```text
ADMIT(A,B)
    iff S1=S2
    and P1=P2
    and q1=q2
    and d1,d2 != 00
    and d1!=d2.
```

When admitted:

```text
FORCE(A,B)=(S1,P1,q1,d1 XOR d2).
```

Therefore `FORCE` is deterministic after admission, but the **full relation remains partial**. Total XOR on either completed subfield does not totalize FIPS.

For standalone Events, represent an edge by `(q,d)` modulo `(q,d)~(q XOR d,d)`. Two distinct Events are cyclically admitted iff their endpoint sets share one block vertex; the shared vertex is unique, and the third Event is obtained by XORing the two incident displacements. This uses equality, XOR, nonzero tests, and endpoint intersection only.
