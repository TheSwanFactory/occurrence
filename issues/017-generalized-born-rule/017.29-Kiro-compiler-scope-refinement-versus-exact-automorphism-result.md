## One-paragraph answer

A frozen fixed-point refinement compiler and the exact automorphism-orbit compiler
were run against each other on 87 preregistered typed worlds, enumerated in an
order computed by a rule rather than written down. They **agreed on 82** of them,
including all 45 worlds whose only primitives are relations, and including both
017.27 fixtures, where the refinement reproduced `|Aut| = 14` with cells
7/14/14/14 and `|Aut| = 6` with cells 3/6/6/6/6 in two rounds and matched the
banked 017.26 cell membership exactly. They came apart on **five**, every one of
which declares a total binary operation. The first separation under the frozen
order is a Latin square of order 6: the refinement puts all **36** admitted inputs
in **one** cell while the autotopism group of order **24** splits them into orbits
of **12 and 24**, and the witness pair `r0c0` against `r0c2` carries three
independent non-existence certificates. Then θ\* was evaluated — by the rule
inherited from 017.24a, only after the structure was banked — and the extra
distinction turned out to matter a lot. The refinement compiler's irreducible
floor is **0.116858121372717375879907** nats, computed in rationals and 40-digit
decimal; the exact compiler's is **exactly zero**; and the exact compiler reached
0.02 excess held-out log loss at **n = 64** while the refinement plateaued at
0.1173 and never came close. Four things temper this. The separation is **rare**:
82 of 87. It is **closable by ordinary source structure** — one declared reference
element takes both compilers to the same 10-cell partition. There is **no
estimation crossover at all**, even at n = 1, which was the axis 017.28 §10 warned
not to presume in either direction and the grid was extended downward specifically
to find one. And on the median world in the family the exact compiler was
**faster**, not slower, so the cost story is about one family and not about
compilation in general.

## A. Compiler definitions

### `Q_ref` — fixed-point pair refinement over the admitted inputs

017.28 §4 refuses to let "pair refinement" carry a comparison and lists nine items
that must be defined first. All nine are in `Q_REF_SPECIFICATION` and emitted
verbatim into the Phase S certificate. The construction:

The **slot pool** of an ordered pair of admitted inputs (x, y) is the typed
components of x in declared signature order, then those of y, then every declared
constant, then — as one closure round — the value of every declared operation on
every type-correct tuple of the slots so far. The **atomic type** τ(x, y) records
which type-correct tuples of slot *positions* satisfy each declared relation and
which same-sorted slot positions hold equal elements. Colours start at τ(x, x) and
refine by

    colour(x) ← ( colour(x), {{ (τ(x, y), colour(y)) : y ∈ X_Σ }} )

to a monotone fixpoint. Cost is |X_Σ|² atomic types plus a bounded number of
O(|X_Σ|² log|X_Σ|) passes — polynomial in |X_Σ| for bounded arity.

The atomic type is a set of slot positions keyed by primitive *declaration index*,
so no name and no element enters it and relabelling invariance holds by
construction as well as by test.

### `Q_aut` — exact automorphism-orbit quotient

`Q_aut(Σ) = X_Σ / Aut(A_Σ)` from the byte-pinned 017.26 compiler, unchanged. Two
exact routes; both were run on every designated world and agreed. `Q_aut` is the
**reference**, not the presumed production compiler.

### The relation between them, verified rather than assumed

Every automorphism preserves every declared fact, so every atomic type is
automorphism-invariant, so `Q_ref` must be no finer than `Q_aut`:

    x ~_aut y  ⇒  x ~_ref y       87 / 87 worlds, plus both replay worlds

`Q_ref` never split an orbit anywhere. §8 makes that a compiler defect, and it did
not occur.

## C. Search preregistration

Six families. The enumeration order is **computed** by a frozen rule, not written
down: families sorted by declared primitive count, then declared sort count, then
name; within a family, parameters sorted by total declared carrier elements, then
by the parameter tuple flattened to integers. The declared tables are therefore
only *sets*. What is checked mechanically is that no two entries tie, so the order
is total, and that each family's declared profile is exactly what its builder
produces.

Two disclosures that belong in the open rather than in a footnote.

**The bounds were set by an off-family probe.** Shuffled and intercalate-turned
cyclic Latin squares, and circulant graphs above the bound, were timed before the
family was frozen, so the bounds reflect compile cost rather than compile results.
No probe world is in `G`: the family's quasigroups are lexicographically reduced
squares, which the probe never generated.

**The family carries a stated hypothesis.** The frozen `Q_ref` reads only the
declared facts of a four-to-eight slot pool, so a world whose declared local data
is constant everywhere is where it should go blind, and a Latin square is the
extreme case. That is why the quasigroup family is in `G`, and it is recorded in
the freeze rather than discovered afterwards. Stating a hypothesis in advance is
preregistration; it is not the same as choosing an instance after seeing the
answer, and the computed ordering rule plus the complete ledger are what make the
difference auditable.

## E. First separation

**`quasigroup:n=6:square=1`** — the second reduced Latin square of order 6 in
lexicographic order.

```text
0 1 2 3 4 5
1 0 3 2 5 4
2 3 4 5 0 1
3 2 5 4 1 0
4 5 0 1 3 2
5 4 1 0 2 3
```

Declared: carriers `Row`(6), `Col`(6), `Sym`(6); one primitive
`op : Row × Col → Sym`, total, 36 rows; admitted inputs `Row × Col`. Nothing else
— no group, no orbit, no equivalence, no number that could be a probability.

| | value |
|---|---|
| derived `\|Aut(A_Σ)\|` | **24**, the autotopism group |
| `Q_aut` | **2 cells**, 12 / 24 |
| `Q_ref` | **1 cell**, 36 |
| `Q_ref` rounds | 1 |
| distinct atomic types | 7 |

### The witness pair

    x = r0c0      y = r0c2

- x and y share refinement cell `R_0`, the only one there is
- x in orbit `A_0` (12 members), y in orbit `A_1` (24 members)
- **no structure-preserving map sends x to y.** The pinned exhaustive search
  exhausts the whole product of symmetric groups under sound pruning and returns
  false, so the negative is exact rather than a search failure
- confirmed a second way: applying **every one of the 24 derived elements** to x
  never reaches y
- confirmed a third way, in the tests, by an algorithm sharing no code with the
  compiler. For a Latin square a choice of row and column bijection *forces* the
  symbol bijection, so the autotopism group can be enumerated outright with no
  backtracking. That independent enumeration returns order 24 and orbit sizes
  12 / 24.

### What the refinement could not see

Two measured facts, both checked mechanically.

**Every cell of this square lies in exactly one intercalate.** On a Latin square
the only non-trivial declared fact the atomic type of a pair of cells can report is
whether the two cells span a 2×2 Latin subsquare. Here that count is 1 for every
cell, so the whole admitted-input set carries one atomic profile and the fixpoint
is one cell after one round.

**The orbit distinction is block agreement.** Writing rows and columns in the three
pairs {0,1}, {2,3}, {4,5}, orbit `A_0` is exactly the cells whose row pair and
column pair coincide. That is a second-order property of the whole square, and the
refinement's update does not compute it. This second fact is diagnostic rather
than load-bearing — the orbit partition is derived from declared structure alone,
and naming the invariant afterwards only makes the separation legible.

## F. Canonicality

**Representation invariance.** Relabelling every carrier, element, and primitive,
and separately stripping every non-structural document key, leaves both compilers'
output identical. Because the relabelling renames positionally and preserves
declared order, the transported partition must be the *identical tuple*, which is
stronger than equality up to renaming. `Q_ref`: 87 / 87 worlds, both tests.
`Q_aut`: on the first world of every family and on the designated fixture, by a
declared rule rather than a per-world choice.

**Algorithmic determinism.** `Q_ref` recomputed in-process on every world:
identical. The whole Phase S certificate recomputed and byte-identical across
`PYTHONHASHSEED` 0, 4242 and `random`; the Phase T reference likewise across two
seeds. That is achievable because the certificate carries **no wall-clock number
and no floating-point measurement of any kind** — every timing lives in the cost
report, which is existence-checked only.

**Mathematical canonicity.** `Q_aut` has an intrinsic characterization: the orbit
partition of the full automorphism group, defined without reference to any
algorithm. `Q_ref` has none. It is canonical only relative to the frozen
construction, and agreement on 82 of 87 worlds does not transfer `Q_aut`'s
canonicity to it.

### Estimation loss — and the honest negative

017.28 §10 says not to presume `A` wins at every sample size, and that a result
where refinement is better throughout the tested regime would be scientifically
important. The inherited grid was extended down to **n = 1 and n = 2** precisely
to look for that regime.

**There is no crossover.** `A` is at or below `R` at every tested size in both
regimes, so the crossover sits at the smallest tested n, and `R` wins nowhere. The
reason is not subtle: `R` pools cells whose true probabilities differ by 0.5, so
its bias term is 0.117 nats while the variance cost of `A`'s one extra parameter
is about 1/(2n) — worth less than a thousandth of a nat by n = 512 and never worth
0.117. A refinement compiler could win this trade on a world where the merged cells
were nearly equal; on this one it cannot, and the grid extension shows that rather
than assuming it.

is the most practically consequential line in this document and it cuts against the
stronger compiler: on this world, the cheapest way to get the exact quotient is not
a better compiler, it is a slightly more explicit schema.

## J. Compiler hierarchy

    local atomic features
        ≤ bounded refinement, one round
        ≤ fixed-point refinement Q_ref
        ≤ exact automorphism orbit Q_aut

Every consecutive pair checked with the refinement relation on every searched
world. The ladder held 87 / 87. Where each step is strict:

| step | strict on |
|---|---|
| bounded over atomic | 64 worlds |
| fixpoint over bounded | 27 worlds |
| **orbits over fixpoint** | **5 worlds** — exactly the separating ones |
| nothing strict | 19 worlds |

This is a measured fact about this declared vocabulary, not a universal total
ordering. Definability quotients, congruence closure, bisimulation-like
equivalence, context equivalence, task-relative observational quotients, and
canonical forms are named in the certificate and implemented nowhere. One of them
— canonical forms — would return the same partition as `Q_aut` by a faster
algorithm and is the obvious next thing to try if the cost column ever binds.

## What is weaker here than the numbers suggest

Five things, stated plainly.

**The separation is rare, and the family was built to find it.** 82 of 87 worlds
agreed. The quasigroup family is in `G` because an a-priori analysis said that is
where the refinement should go blind — and that analysis was only partly right,
because 24 of 28 quasigroup worlds agreed too. A reader should take "5 of 87" as
the headline and not "a separation exists".

**One declared element closes the gap.** Adding a reference row — ordinary source
structure — takes both compilers to the same 10-cell partition. On this world the
cheapest route to the exact quotient is a more explicit schema, not a stronger
compiler. That is a real finding and it argues against paying for `Q_aut`.

**There is no estimation trade-off to report.** §10 asked for a crossover and the
grid was extended to n = 1 to find one. There is none, because the merged cells
differ by 0.5 in probability. A world with nearly equal merged cells would very
likely invert this, and none was tested.

**The exact compiler was usually faster.** Median time ratio 0.82 across the
family. The 627× worst case is real and it is entirely inside the typed-operation
worlds. Any claim that exact compilation is expensive has to name the family it is
expensive on.

**One world, 36 admitted inputs, two orbits.** The statistical half of this turn
rests on a single Latin square of order 6 with a two-parameter truth, chosen by a
frozen order rather than for its size. It is not representative of anything beyond
itself.

## What this makes the next question

017.28 §21 says the next question depends on the verdict. On a useful separation it
is:

> **What tractable compiler approximates the stronger quotient without paying
> exact global-symmetry cost?**

This turn hands that question three concrete starting points it did not have.
Canonical labelling returns `Q_aut`'s partition by a different algorithm and is
untried here. The separation lives entirely in worlds declaring a total binary
operation, which is a narrow enough target to attack directly. And the cheapest
fix found on the separation fixture was not a compiler at all — it was one more
declared element, which suggests a fourth question worth asking beside the third:

> **Which small additions to a Type Schema make cheap refinement sufficient?**
