## One-paragraph answer

A generic compiler that reads only a primitive's declared **kind and signature** derived `|Aut| = 14` with input cells 7/14/14/14 on a single-relation world and `|Aut| = 6` with cells 3/6/6/6/6 on a two-primitive world, from schemas that declare no group, no generator, no orbit, and no equivalence — and it did so in a module that cannot have seen a label, because it does not import the module containing the label generator. That derivation was committed and banked **before** `θ*` was chosen. Once labels arrived, the derived quotient reached 0.02 excess held-out log loss at **n = 128** on both worlds, while the preregistered shortcut learner `R` never reached it on either, plateauing at **0.0416** against its exactly predicted floor of **0.040527** on Fixture A and **0.0554** against **0.054445** on Fixture B. The best of six uncompiled learners never reached 0.02 on Fixture A and first reached it at **n = 1024** on Fixture B, an eightfold labelled-data difference. The handed-quotient oracle, reading cell membership out of the committed freeze by a path that never calls the compiler, produced **bit-identical predictions in all 14,400 replicate comparisons**. Every one of the 135 frozen regime-A predictions reproduced within four standard errors, worst 3.38σ, zero failures — including the prediction that cut against the thesis, that the two-parameter `R` would beat `D` at n = 4 on Fixture A and there only. Three things temper this. The result is about two finite worlds of 49 and 27 admitted inputs. The comparison is against tested learners, not against a lower bound. And the Phase S freeze itself disclosed that a cheaper **structural** derivation — an iterated pair refinement — recovers both quotients in two iterations, so `Q_aut` is not the cheapest route to its own answer, only a correct and exactly certifiable one.

## C. Derived structure — what follows from preservation alone

The rule table is the whole semantic content of the word *generic*:

- relation `R ⊆ C₁ × ⋯ × Cₙ`: `R(a₁,…,aₙ) ⇔ R(g_{C₁}(a₁),…,g_{Cₙ}(aₙ))`
- operation `f : C₁ × ⋯ × Cₙ → B`: `g_B(f(a₁,…,aₙ)) = f(g_{C₁}(a₁),…,g_{Cₙ}(aₙ))`
- constant `c : C`: `g_C(c) = c`

| | Fixture A | Fixture B |
|---|---|---|
| product of symmetric groups searched | 25,401,600 | 2,177,280 |
| `\|Aut(A_Σ)\|` | **14** | **6** |
| `Q_aut` cells | **4** — 7/14/14/14 | **5** — 3/6/6/6/6 |
| derive time | 0.010 s | 0.008 s |

On Fixture A the derived quotient **strictly refines** the declared relation: the relation splits the inputs 21/28, every derived cell lies wholly inside or wholly outside it, 21 splitting 7 + 14 and 28 splitting 14 + 14. That is 017.25a's separation, reached without a single handed generator.

Maximality is argued in layers, not asserted. Every returned element is re-verified by an independent checker that re-derives the rules from the signature. Unpruned brute force over the whole product on a reduced world returns the **same element set** (576 candidates on A, 1,440 on B). A second exhaustive decomposition — enumerate one carrier outright, complete the rest — returns the **same order**. Composing every derived element with every transposition exhausts Cayley distance one: **0 of 588** and **0 of 234** neighbours preserve every primitive. And the quotient is derived twice, by pair query and by union-find over the materialised group, and the two agree. The tests re-derive both orders by a second algorithm not shared with the compiler, including an exhaustive walk over all 362,880 permutations of `Site`.

## D. Cheap-statistic challenge — and its bound

017.26b §4 replaces "cheap statistic" with a preregistered finite language. `L_k` is generated mechanically at **k = 2** from the declared primitives and enumerated exhaustively.

| | Fixture A | Fixture B |

| `\|L_k\|` | 5 | 18 |

| **whole language read jointly** | **21 / 28** | **9 / 18** |
| recovers `q_Σ` | **no** | **no** |

Two strengthenings over the letter of §4. Raw count values are kept as features, not only the equality tests between them. And the **joint** partition is reported, which bounds the entire Boolean closure without enumerating it: any negation, conjunction, or disjunction of these atoms induces a partition coarser than or equal to their joint. A test checks directly that every `L_k` feature is invariant under every derived map, which is what makes "does not recover" mean exactly "is strictly coarser".

A fact about Fixture B worth stating rather than burying: the frozen `L_k` partition turns out to be **exactly** the quotient that survives erasing `adjacent`. So on that world the cheapest preregistered statistic is precisely what one primitive alone can support, and the derived quotient is what joint preservation adds on top. Conditions `R` and `P_without_adjacent` are consequently the same condition, and their measured curves are identical to every digit.

### The bound, banked at the freeze rather than caveated afterwards

017.26b §4.1 requires the bound on this claim to be named. Naming it is not enough, so the stronger adversary was implemented in Phase S and its result committed before `θ*` existed:

> An iterated **pair refinement** over the admitted inputs — colours seeded by an input's own declared facts, refined by the multiset over every other input of the facts the pair jointly satisfies — **recovers `q_Σ` exactly on both fixtures, in two iterations**.

Three things follow, and no more. It is not a member of `L_k`, which is source-local and depth-bounded by construction, so `L_k`'s result stands. 017.26b §8 forbids `R` from computing "an equivalent global structural quotient", which is precisely what this is, so `R` is correctly fenced from it by the contract rather than by convenience. And it does not make the derivation cheaper *in kind*, because it is itself a deterministic pre-label structural derivation and not a statistic a learner reads off an input — it makes it cheaper *in cost*. That is a tractability finding, and it speaks directly to the 017.26b §12 open item about whether `Q_aut` is the compiler to keep.

## I. Counterfactual schemas — does the compiler follow the world

Directions were frozen in the module and committed before any perturbed world was compiled. Every perturbed world was recompiled from scratch through the same generic path. **All eight held.**

Both symmetry breaks yield a **subgroup** by set inclusion and a **refinement** of the quotient, which is §6's `Aut(A_Σ⁺) ⊆ Aut(A_Σ)` prediction. A scramble preserves the carriers, the row count, and the fibre-size multiset and still destroys the quotient.

**Joint load-bearing.** Erasing `adjacent` takes `|Aut|` from 6 to 1,296 and the quotient from 5 cells to 2. Erasing `district` takes it to 108 and to 1 cell. Each primitive strictly enlarges the group **and** strictly coarsens the quotient when removed, so neither is decoration, and 017.26b §5's requirement for a Strong positive is met by measurement. Fixture A has one primitive and therefore could not have earned a Strong positive alone, exactly as the contract says.

**Label independence.** The strongest available form, and it is structural: the Phase S module does not import the byte-pinned 017.24 implementation, because that module contains `generate_observations`. No label could have been consulted even by accident, and the import list is recorded in the certificate.

## What is weaker here than the numbers suggest

Four things, stated plainly.

**The cheapest correct compiler is not this one.** The pair refinement recovers both quotients in two iterations of polynomial work, against an exhaustive search over a product of symmetric groups. On these worlds `Q_aut` is a correct and exactly certifiable route to its answer, not an efficient one. If the next question is compiler scope, that is where it starts.

**One measured ordering did not resolve.** At n = 4 on Fixture B the exact reference predicts `D` ahead of `R`, 0.1343 against 0.1382, and the measured means invert it, 0.1270 against 0.1254. Both cells deviate from their exact values in the same direction by about 3σ — these are the two largest deviations in the run — because the same observations drive both conditions, so their errors are correlated. The confidence intervals overlap, `[0.1224, 0.1317]` against `[0.1180, 0.1328]`. The n = 4 ordering on Fixture B is therefore not established either way, and the banked prediction should be read as unconfirmed at that one cell rather than as confirmed or refuted.

**Fixture A is a restatement.** Its relation is 017.24a's relation. What is new about it is that the symmetry was derived rather than declared — a real change — but the world itself is not independent evidence, and the contract is right that it could not have carried a Strong positive alone.

**Two finite worlds.** 49 and 27 admitted inputs, chosen for tractability and for having the structural property under test. Neither is representative of anything beyond itself.

## What this makes the next question

017.26b §13 says that on a Strong positive the next research problem is compiler scope:

> **Which useful Type Schemas admit tractable, canonical pre-label structural compilation, and what representation burden can thereby be removed from Training?**

This turn hands that question two concrete starting points it did not have before. The first is that the expensive compiler is not the only one that works: a pair refinement got the same answer on both worlds, in polynomial time, and the interesting question is which worlds separate the two. The second is that the slogan that survives is narrower than *types make learning cheaper* and narrower than *OT makes learning cheaper*:

> **A sufficiently explicit world has transformations that preserve it, and those transformations induce distinctions a learner need not infer from labels.**

That is what was measured. Nothing more was.
