## 1. Answer

Yes, on the split that actually tests composition. With a caveat that matters
more than the headline.

```text
primary split: motif (withholds sign patterns 011 and 100)

learned structural policy, held-out exact-native success   0.7595  (0.966 / 0.322 / 0.991)
best deployable deterministic baseline                     0.1377  (RandomLegalTree)
margin                                                    +0.6218
seeds materially beating the bar                            3 of 3
availability ceiling over the input distribution            0.1489
```

The caveat: **every structured deterministic rule scores below chance on this
split**, so the bar is set by the random legal selector at 0.1377. GroupedFirst
reaches 0.0171, ForceSeq 0.0085, the train-fitted availability rule 0.0057. The
honest statement is not "the policy beat a strong rule" but "the policy went
from chance to 0.76 on held-out compositions where no deterministic rule reaches
chance at all."

And the second caveat, which is the more interesting finding: on any split that
does **not** withhold whole sign patterns, a deterministic eight-entry lookup
table scores exactly 1.000, so no learned policy can materially exceed it. See
section 5.

## 3. Frozen task

Unchanged from `008.02`, per `008.04` section 4. No exact defect was found in
the target relation.

```text
signature      E^3 R
constructors   Occ + Cyc + Sand
retained       r = e4
denotations    true / frozen, 84 certified basic Events
programs       20 strict planar terms
target         bits  = 4*b(s1) + 2*b(s2) + b(s3)
               index = bits mod 20
```

Because `bits < 8 < 20` the modulus is inert and the reachable pool is exactly
the eight `Cyc`-free bracketings. In the `programs.py` enumeration order, bit `d`
of `bits` sets whether the retained head at depth `d` is `Sand`. Bit 0 is the
sign of the **last** Event and governs the **outermost** head, so the relation is
a reversal, not a per-slot copy — the policy has to discover the crossing.

Firewall, checked mechanically rather than asserted: the target does not inspect
model scores, uses no decoder, and is not an availability rule — inputs sharing
one availability pattern demand different programs, which is what
`test_target_is_not_an_availability_rule` verifies.

The learner sees 64 numbers: the exact rational coordinates of the three Event
denotations and the retained context, cast to float. Nothing else. It emits a
score over the legal programs; the selected program is executed by the exact
evaluator.

## 4. Baselines

Eight, on both halves of every split. Held-out half of the primary `motif` split:

| Baseline | Deployable | test |
|---|:--:|---:|
| `SuppliedBracketingExecutor` | no — handed the answer's tree | 1.0000 |
| `AvailabilityOracleCeiling` | no — fitted on the scored half | 0.5203 |
| **`RandomLegalTree`** | **yes** | **0.1377** |
| `GroupedFirst` | yes | 0.0171 |
| `ForceSeq` | yes | 0.0085 |
| `MajorityProgramTrainFitted` | yes | 0.0059 |
| `SignPatternLookupTrainFitted` | yes | 0.0059 |
| `AvailabilityRuleTrainFitted` | yes | 0.0057 |

Two of these are bounds, not competitors, and the report keeps them separate.
The supplied-bracketing executor is the `H_weak` reference and cannot miss. The
availability oracle is a per-pattern argmax fitted on the labels of the half it
is scored on; it reaches 0.5203 here only because the held-out half contains
just two sign patterns, so a rule with access to held-out labels can split the
difference between them. Over the full input distribution the same family is
bounded by 0.1489. The learned structural policy exceeds even this
non-deployable oracle, on 2 of 3 seeds and in the mean.

Every structured deterministic rule lands *below* the random legal selector,
which is worth stating plainly: they each commit to a program that is never the
held-out target, whereas a uniform draw over roughly eight legal alternatives
still lands on it about one time in eight.

## 5. The finding that constrains the whole benchmark

The `008.02` target is a function of the three Event sign bits alone. There are
eight sign patterns. So a deterministic lookup table with eight entries, fitted
on train targets and reading nothing but an allowed input feature, reproduces
the target exactly wherever its pattern was seen in training.

Measured, held-out half, `SignPatternLookupTrainFitted`:

```text
random split          1.0000
unseen_events split   1.0000
motif split           0.0059
motif_parity split    0.0047
```

Consequences:

- The i.i.d. split and the held-out-Event split **cannot** support a learned
  composition claim. The learned policy scores 1.0000 on both, and so does the
  lookup table, so the margin is exactly 0.0000. Under `008.04` section 9 those
  are negative for the intended claim even though absolute accuracy is perfect,
  and they are reported as controls.
- Held-out *combinations* are not sufficient. `008.04` section 6 asks for
  held-out combinations and/or bracketing motifs and prefers a compositional
  split; on this target only the stronger reading works. A split must withhold
  whole **sign patterns**, because the pattern is the entire input signature of
  the target.
- The `unseen_events` result is still worth having, just not as a claim. The
  policy reaches 1.0000 on triples containing Events it never saw, which shows it
  reads Event structure rather than memorizing Event identities — the sign bit is
  the sum of coordinates 8..15, a linear functional available on any certified
  Event.

This is the same shape of problem `008.02` section 4a.3 reported one level up:
the section-4 firewall was necessary but not sufficient, and a joint-domain check
had to be added. Here the section-6 split requirement is necessary but not
sufficient, and a "withhold whole target-determining patterns" check has to be
added.

Per held-out pattern, structural on `motif`:

pattern 011 -> Sand(a1,Sand(a2,Occ(a3,r)))   0.9323 / 0.0700 / 0.9852
pattern 100 -> Occ(a1,Occ(a2,Sand(a3,r)))    0.9998 / 0.5739 / 0.9960

The seed spread is not noise around one number. Seeds 0 and 2 essentially solve

section 9 "survives more than one seed" requirement is met — but the instability
is real and should be read as part of the result rather than smoothed away.

`motif_parity` being *easier* than `motif` (1.000 on every seed, against 0.76) is
unexplained. It withholds twice as many motifs and leaves training on a linear
subspace of sign words, so the naive expectation is the opposite. Two candidate
explanations — a tighter constraint relative to capacity, or a more balanced
training signal — are both untested, and this turn does not adjudicate them.

are reported separately anyway. And legality masking contributes nothing: the
unmasked argmax already selects a defined program on every held-out input, so the
result is not an artifact of masking away the hard choices.

## 7. Ladder C — ambient generalized `Mul`

Same twenty tree shapes, every node evaluated with unguarded ambient sedenion
multiplication: the strict `Cyc` admissibility precondition — 336 certified
ordered pairs out of 7056 — and the Event-role guard are discarded. Everything
else, including the exact arithmetic, is unchanged.

Verified before use: ambient and native agree exactly on all eight `Cyc`-free
bracketings, so the target endpoint is identical under both semantics. The
control changes availability, not the relation. Mean legal programs per input
goes from 8.04 native to 18.95 ambient.

| Arm | Split | ambient test | rescored natively |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| flat | `motif` | 0.0015 | 0.0015 |
| structural | `motif` | **0.4220** | **0.4220** |
| flat | `random` | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |
| structural | `random` | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |

Ambient per seed on `motif`: 0.4308 / 0.0085 / 0.8266.

Discarding the strict domain **hurts**: 0.7595 native against 0.4220 ambient on
the primary split, and the ambient runs are more unstable. The native rescoring
equals the ambient score exactly, because the ambient policy's chosen program is
natively defined on 100 % of held-out inputs — it never actually exploits the
ambient-only domain, it just faces twelve more distractors.

Read narrowly, which is all this licenses: on this task the strict availability
structure carries information a learner can use, even though availability alone
predicts the target at only 0.1489. It does **not** license enlarging the strict
OT domain, and an ambient tree is still not an OT occurrence.

## 8. Reported problems — for Owner disposition

### 8.1 A defect in the first split this experimentalist built, corrected before training

The first `motif` split withheld the homogeneous sign patterns `000` and `111`.
That is wrong, and `008.04` section 4 says why: the `000` target is
`Occ(a1,Occ(a2,Occ(a3,r)))`, which **is** the pure right comb, so ForceSeq
answers half the held-out set correctly by construction. Measured before the
correction: ForceSeq 0.5016, GroupedFirst 0.4655 on that held-out half.

Corrected to withhold `011` and `100`, whose targets are neither the right comb
nor a most-grouped term. Both constraints are now asserted in the split metadata
and pinned by `test_motif_split_does_not_hand_the_answer_to_a_fixed_executor`.

Reported rather than quietly fixed because the failure mode is not specific to
this experiment: on a target whose reachable pool is the `Cyc`-free bracketings,
the extreme patterns are exactly the canonical fixed executors, so "withhold the
extremes" is the intuitive choice and the wrong one.

**No Owner decision needed.** Recorded for the next person who builds a split on
this target.

### 8.2 `008.04` section 6 is necessary but not sufficient — Owner decision needed

Section 6 requires held-out combinations and/or bracketing motifs and prefers a
compositional split. On the `008.02` target that is not enough, because the
target is a function of a three-bit input signature with only eight values: any
split that leaves all eight patterns in training is solved exactly by an
eight-entry deterministic lookup table, measured at 1.000 on two of the four
splits here.

`SignPatternLookupTrainFitted` is not in the `008.01` section 8 baseline list. It
was added under `008.04` section 5.B, which requires adding any deterministic
rule exposed during implementation that uses only allowed input or availability
features. It is the strongest deterministic baseline on two of four splits, by a
margin of 0.85.

Owner decision needed: whether the required construction gains a third check
alongside the section-4 firewall and the `008.03` section 2.3 viability gate —
something like *the split must withhold whole values of whatever input signature
determines the target*, together with a requirement to report a fitted lookup
baseline over that signature. Without it, a future `008` result could report
1.000 held-out accuracy on an i.i.d. split and mean nothing.

## 9. Stop-rule accounting

`008.04` section 10 allows one principled architecture pass plus at most one
clearly motivated repair.

**Pass 1 — `FlatPolicy`, 2740 parameters.** MLP over the input coordinates, one
free logit per program, masked to the legal set. The direct generalization of the
`017.09` `Policy` head from two branches to twenty. Result: perfect train,
0.0018 held-out on the primary split.

**The repair — `StructuralPolicy`, 2773 parameters.** Motivated by a diagnosable
structural defect, not by tuning. On a held-out-motif split two of the twenty
program labels never appear as a training target, so the flat head's output
columns for them receive no gradient that could make them win; its held-out score
is bounded by the rate at which a *seen* program happens to share the unseen
target's endpoint, which is near zero. The fix removes the free per-program
column: `score(p | x) = f(x) · phi(p)` with `phi` a fixed, target-agnostic
term-calculus encoding, so a program is scored only through parts it shares with
other programs.

`phi` is a registered buffer, never trained, fixed before training, mentions no
sign bit, no availability, no endpoint and no target, and is checked injective
over the twenty programs. Two of its twenty-one features exist only to separate
`Cyc(Cyc(a1,a2),a3)` from `Cyc(a1,Cyc(a2,a3))`; without them the encoding
collapses those two terms and the head cannot tell them apart. It encodes the
grammar of the program space, which is public structure, not the relation being
learned — the policy still has to discover that the sign of the last Event
governs the outermost head.

**Stop.** No further tuning, no hyperparameter search, no third architecture.
Returning the result.

## 10. Disposition

**Positive**, on the primary compositional split, under the `008.04` section 9
rule: held-out exact-native success 0.7595 against 0.1377 for the best deployable
deterministic baseline, a margin of +0.6218, material on 3 of 3 seeds. The
structural policy also exceeds the non-deployable within-split availability
oracle at 0.5203.

Negative, and reported as such, on `random` and `unseen_events`: absolute
accuracy 1.0000, margin 0.0000, because a deterministic lookup table already
scores 1.0000 there.

The limitation this turn exposes is **task construction**, in the `008.01`
section 10 vocabulary — specifically split construction. Strict program-space
separability is not the limit; `008.02` already fixed that by admitting `Sand`.
Policy learning is not the limit either, except for the one-seed-in-three
instability recorded in section 6.

### Ladder D

**Warranted.** `008.04` section 5.D gates recovered denotations on Ladder A
materially beating the relevant deterministic baselines, which it does on the
primary split.

Three recommendations if Ladder D is opened:

1. Run it on `motif` and `motif_parity` only. The i.i.d. and held-out-Event
   splits cannot discriminate, and a Ladder-D result reported on them would be
   uninterpretable for the same reason Ladder A's is.
2. Carry `SignPatternLookupTrainFitted` forward. A recovered-denotation arm that
   ties it has shown nothing.
3. Fix the seed instability first, or report it. A 0.322 seed against two seeds
   near 0.99 will be indistinguishable from a Ladder-D degradation unless the
   Ladder-A variance is characterized. Three seeds are not enough to separate
   those.
